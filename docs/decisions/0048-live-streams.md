# ADR 0048: Live streams

Status: Accepted (2026-10-27). Home: `internal/layer2/gamedata` (the stream data and its check), `internal/layer3/broadcast` (the rules), `internal/rpg` (the progress in the save), `internal/react` (the additions to the reaction book), `internal/play` (the lobby and the watch view).

## Context

ADR 0015 named a streamer game as the first user of `react` and `chat`, but no screen uses them: `chat` has no importer outside tests and `react` is reached only through the `respond` command inside a novel event. A streamer game shows several channels at once, each a moving portrait in its own room speaking lines that advance with the wall clock, with a chat that flows, free comments the streamer answers, tips, and questions the viewers answer. None of the existing screens advances several scripts at once by time, and the novel's single event flow, rollback and read marks (ADR 0025, ADR 0026) do not fit lines that keep going whether or not anyone watches.

## Decision

Add a live-stream screen, opened from a location (`stream: lobby`) or with the command `open: streams`. It has two views: the lobby, which shows every live channel as a card across the width of the screen (the most watched first, as on a streaming site, the order kept until the live shows change: a large first card with its chat beside it, the others smaller, four to a row, scrolled to the chosen card) and after the cards a small row of the channels offline, and the watch view of one show. A lobby that is a location shows the place's entries (its exits, actions, people and the menu) as small buttons in the bar over the lobby, between the game's title and the gold, each with a › for it leads to another screen and the settings' (an action that runs `open: config`) with a gear, chosen after the cards with the keys and run as a field runs them; the settings opened over the streams are a page under the stream bar, whose way back (‹ and the lobby's name) closes them; the menu key opens the menu (neither the menu entry nor the key with `menus: none`, ADR 0049); an opened lobby goes back with cancel. The lobby has no list of the channels beside the cards: the cards are the list, and the space goes to them. Every card shows its show's line as a caption on its picture, the line the watch view would show, kept over the chat beats after it for 1.5 seconds from the line's end and then taken away.

### Data (written by the author, checked by `gozen check`)

`game.yaml` names one file with `streams: data/streams.yaml` (schema version 9):

```yaml
logo: logo.png                                            # under assets/, before the title in the bar (a colored square without it)
timing: {say: 1.8, per_char: 0.11, chat: 1.6, ask: 12}   # seconds a beat lasts
live: {share: 0.8, min_share: 0.6, duo_chance: 0.25}      # who is live when the game opens; everyone on the first opening
away: {per_hour: 1500, cap_hours: 24, star_bonus: 0.1}    # gold earned while the game is closed
tips: [500, 2000, 10000, 50000]
affection: {steps: [0, 30, 150, 600, 3000], comment: 1, reply: 2, answer: 5, tip_per: 100, stayed: 10}
answer_chance: [0.6, 0.75, 0.9, 1, 1]                     # by affection step
echo: {max_runes: 24, chance: 0.35}                       # reading back a comment the book found nothing in
bounce: {on: "[!！]|[wW]{2}", amount: 1}                  # the lines that jiggle the puppet
crowd:                                                    # bot viewers (chat.Crowd)
  names: [...]
  lines: {any: [...], morning: [...], evening: [...]}     # or a plain list (any)
  tip_lines: [...]
  rate: 0.4
channels:
  - id: lise
    actor: lise              # the actor's name, puppet (ADR 0020) and reaction book (reactions:)
    tag: Games
    tagline: A line about the channel
    color: "#c0392b"         # the name in the chat, the card's frame
    room: rooms/lise.jpg     # under assets/, one per channel
    frame: {cx: 575, top: 230, bottom: 660}   # the part of the puppet shown, in puppet pixels
    voice: {pitch: 0.9, rate: 0.95}           # speech synthesis in a browser build
    episodes: [streams/lise/001.yaml, streams/lise/002.yaml]   # relative to the game
    greet: [[], ["{from}, you came back."], ...]   # one list of lines per affection step
    first: [...]
    streak: [...]            # {days} is the run of days in a row
    stayed: [...]
duos:
  - {host: lise, with: mora, episodes: [streams/lise-mora/001.yaml]}
```

`affection`, `answer_chance` and `echo` have the defaults shown; `bounce` without `on` jiggles nothing. The episodes live under `streams/` of the game, outside `data/` (the files under `data/` are data files, and the streams file is read apart from them); an export copies the YAML files under `streams/` as it copies `data/`. An episode is `title`, `lobby` (short lines shown in turn under the show's card; the channel's tagline, or its tag, when there are none: a card never shows the title) and `parts`, each a list of beats: `say: text` or `say: {face, text}` for the host, `<channel id>: text` (or `{face, text}`) for the other host of a duo, `chat: [name, text]`, and `ask: {who, q, options: [{c, r: [lines]}], npc: [[name, option]]}` with the option counted from 0. In every line `{from}` is whom the line is said to: the viewer in greetings, the one who answered in the lines of an answer. The check reports unknown channels, actors (an actor needs a puppet), faces, speakers who are not hosts of the episode, rooms, out-of-range answers, options outside 2 to 4, a question without a bot answer, unknown `{fields}`, affection steps that do not start at 0 and increase, an answer chance missing for a step, more greetings than steps, a broken `bounce.on`, episodes shorter than 10 minutes (warning GZN0118), and, in the layout check, lines that take more than two lines of the caption (GZN0101). The titles, lobby lines, lines, chat messages and viewer names, questions, answers, tags, taglines and crowd texts are texts to translate (ADR 0027).

Comments go to the host's `react` person (`reactions:` in `game.yaml`, ADR 0015) as a `say` stimulus with the comment's text; tips as a stimulus of the kind `tip` with `value.amount` and the tip's comment as its text. A comment the book found nothing in (the person's `dodge.unknown`) is read back only when it has at most `echo.max_runes` characters, with the chance `echo.chance`. A tip with a comment first has the streamer read the comment in quotes (corner brackets in Japanese, double quotes otherwise); the bots tip now and then (an amount among the two smallest `tips`, a `tip_lines` comment) and a host reads it the same way. To answer comments in two languages without false matches, `react` gains, all in the book:

- a lexicon word may be `{word: cat, whole: true}`: matched in the text as written, ignoring case, between word boundaries (for Latin words);
- a lexicon word may be `{word: WORD, exact: true}`: matched in the text as written, ignoring only case (a katakana word that the normalization would turn into unrelated words);
- a tag may be `{words: [...], except: [...]}`: an occurrence of a word inside an occurrence of an except word is not counted, while another occurrence of a word still is;
- a rule's `say` entry may be a list of lines spoken in order, and `{text}` inserts the comment.

### Rules (`internal/layer3/broadcast`, no drawing)

The beat at a time is found from the seconds since the episode started with the timing above (a question lasts its line, `timing.ask` and its longest answer), so the lobby cards and the watch view show the same line, and a show opened late starts in the middle (at the next beat after a question). Opening the game decides who is live: every channel on the first opening, then each with probability `share` and at least `min_share` of them in the order written, a duo when both hosts are live with probability `duo_chance`. A duo streams only once it is unlocked: each of its hosts' own streams watched for its `unlock` minutes (30 when left out; the time each channel's own streams were watched is kept in the save as `watched`). With `slot.rest`, when a channel's stream ends, an unlocked duo of it whose other host streams on its own then takes both their places by the chance `duo_chance` (the lobby shows the duo's card in place of the two; a viewer watching the other host's stream sees it to its end in the watch view), and when the duo ends each host streams on its own again; so one streamer is never in two streams at once. With `slot.rest: 0` (a channel's next stream starts as its stream ends) the duo comes in the same way, at the end of a stream. A duo's card has a mark ("Collab") after its viewers, so a duo just unlocked is seen at once. A guest (`guest: true` on a channel) has no stream or card of its own and is never listed offline: it streams only as the `with` of a duo, unlocked by its host's streams alone; a flag on a channel rather than a list of its own, as a guest has all a channel has for a stream (actor, puppet, frame, color, voices) but its episodes and room. Each show started up to four minutes before the game opened, and before its middle. A channel (or a duo) plays the next episode the player has not seen; watching 60% of the beats from where the player came in marks it seen. A stream plays a slot of episodes one after another ([ADR 0052](0052-stream-voices-slots-and-chat.md)). The affection step, the answer chance, the days-in-a-row count (from 3 days a streak line is said) and the away gold (`per_hour` for at most `cap_hours`, raised by `star_bonus` for every affection step of every channel) are computed here. The clock and the random source are passed in, so the rules run headless and are table-tested.

### State

`rpg.State` gains `Streams`: the viewer's name, per channel the next episode, affection points, the last day visited and the run of days, per duo its next episode, plus the time the game was last open. Saved like the rest; old saves load with it empty, and a save whose next episode of a channel or a duo is below 0 or above 2^30 does not load (the episodes are taken round from it). The game's starting gold is `start.gold` of `game.yaml`.

### Screen (`internal/play`)

The cards and the watch view draw the channel's room and its puppet's frame into a sub-image, the frame filling the height; a duo frames both hosts 1.25 times as tall, side by side. Each place that shows a puppet has its own body made from the shared rig, so a channel's card and its watch view move apart, and a stream watched goes on in the watch view after the lobby's card shows a duo of its host. The chat is a `chat.Log` with the crowd (`chat.Crowd`, its moods the hours of the crowd's lines) as its source; each message is wrapped once when posted, and only the visible messages are drawn. A streamer's message has a mark and the channel's color; the viewers' names have colors of their own. The decorations of the stream screens (the title in the bar, the LIVE marks, the viewers, the hosts' names on the cards and in the watch view, and a name plate at the bottom left of the stage with the hosts' names and the host's tag on a band of the host's color, which the caption stays above) are drawn in `fonts.pop` when the game has one, glyph by glyph where it has the glyph and in the regular font where not (`gfx.Pop`); the captions, the chat and the buttons stay in the regular font, and text all ASCII elsewhere takes `fonts.pop` as before. The caption is white text on black bands under the stage, the whole line at once when its beat begins, with the speaker's name over it in a duo. A caption is shown for at least 2.5 seconds before the next step, however short the line's timing, its voice or its speech, so a short line is not gone before it is read; the watch view lags the stream's clock for it as it does for a voice (ADR 0052), while the stream's clock (`internal/layer3/broadcast`) and the lobby's cards keep the timing of `streams.yaml`. When a chat beat follows the line, its caption is taken away 1.5 seconds after that beat begins (the lobby's cards: 1.5 seconds after the line's end), not kept over a run of chat beats. A line that matches `bounce.on`, and a tip, jiggle the puppet. The comment field is the memo's text field, moved into a shared type used by both; the lobby's first opening asks the viewer's name with it, in a small box over the cards that takes the keys until the name is given (empty for a nameless viewer), so a show never opens without one; under its hint the box says, a line each, that the name cannot be changed later (nothing changes it) and, when `away.per_hour` is above 0, that gold piles up while the game is closed. The watch view has a full screen, switched, as on a video site, with a round see-through button at the bottom right of the stage (four corners pointing out, or in to come back; 44 units square for a finger, clear of the caption, which stays above it, of the name plate and of the chat over the stage in full screen), also reached with the keys among the controls, or the hide key (V): the room and the puppets fill the screen at 16:9 (the stage stays 16:9 in every layout, so a full screen shows the same part of the puppets as the page), the page's bar and the information under the stage are left out, the chat runs over the right of the stage on a darkened band with light text, the answers, the comment field and the buttons stay at its bottom right, the caption stays at the bottom centre clear of the chat, and a small button at the top left goes back to the lobby. The choice is a setting (`stream_full` in the settings file), kept for every save. A game that offers more than one language (`languages`, ADR 0027) has a button for each in the bar of both views, side by side before the entries, the one shown filled; a tap shows the game in that language at once as the settings' language row does, keeps it in the settings, has the crowds chat in it and marks the streamers' messages in it. The chat column has the question's answer cards, the comment field, the tip button with the amounts the player can pay, and a button that reads the lines aloud, from their recorded voices ([ADR 0052](0052-stream-voices-slots-and-chat.md)) or with a browser's `speechSynthesis`, and waits for each line to end (a desktop build without voices has no such button). The tips open over the chat with a field for the tip's message above their amounts: the comment field's text, shared ("Message (optional)" when empty), typed in at once, an amount chosen with the keys or the pad and sent with OK (Enter), or tapped, and closed with Cancel (Escape) keeping the text; a finger on the field opens a phone's keyboard. A tip's message is cut at 40 characters, a comment's at 60: a tip's is shown in one line on its band and kept over the chat, and read aloud before the host answers it. A tip, the player's or a bot's, is made to stand out: a band of its tier's color (the tiers are the first four amounts of `tips`) with who sent it and the amount in the pop font and the comment under them over the top of the stage, centred where the caption is and clear of the LIVE mark, the name plate and the caption, for 5 seconds, the tips after it in turn; the built-in sound `tip`, which a game replaces with `assets/sfx/tip.ogg` as any sound, so no setting is added; a tip of the third tier kept over the chat for 60 seconds and of the fourth for 120, the latest one shown; and the host reads it out with the face `happy`, else `laugh`, when its puppet has one. A show that ends thanks a viewer who watched most of it by name and then shows that it ended. The watch view goes at its own pace (voices, the time a caption is read), ahead of the stream's clock at times: a show left and opened again while the game is open comes in at the later of the clock's beat and the beat it was left at, so what was heard is not said again (a show's start is drawn again at every opening, so nothing of it is saved). The lobby rebuilds its cards only when the live set changes, a channel still on the air keeping its card and its viewers and the card chosen staying the same channel's; a finger that touches the lobby or the chat while it glides after a swipe stops it, and its lifting opens nothing, and shows the gold earned while away once. The progress is autosaved when an episode is seen, after a tip, when the name is given and when the player leaves a show; and so that the time the game was open does not count as time away, the autosave is written with the time the game is open every minute while the game rests on an idle screen, or the watch view, the menu or the settings over it (none of them changes the state; another screen, a battle or an event waits for the next), when the game closes there, and when a browser hides the page (its frames stop, and it may be closed unseen), not on every tick.

The game reads the clock once, when a game with streams starts (`Options.Now`, the wall clock when nil), and advances it by ticks. The clock keeps the offset from UTC it was read at for the whole run, across a change of summer time too. A trace records the time read with that offset and a replay plays the streams at that time and offset, whatever the zone of the machine replaying it, so a replay is still deterministic (the days of a visit and the hour of the crowd and of `value.hour` included). Text typed into the comment field is not part of a replay (as for the memo).

## Consequences

A streamer game is YAML only. The chat, the reactions and the puppets are the existing packages; the new code is the stream data and check, the stream rules, and the two views. The stream screens choose their size and a portrait or landscape layout from the window ([ADR 0050](0050-stream-screens-fit-the-window.md)); the other screens stay in the game's fixed logical size. The content of a particular game (its scripts, its words) stays in that game, not in this repository (ADR 0004).
