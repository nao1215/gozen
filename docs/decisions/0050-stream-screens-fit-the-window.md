# ADR 0050: Stream screens fit the window

Status: Accepted (2026-10-27). Home: `internal/play` (the size the screen is drawn at, the stream screens' layouts, touch scrolling).

## Context

A game is drawn at the fixed logical size of `game.yaml` `screen`, scaled to the window. The stream screens of ADR 0048 are mostly watched in a phone's browser, held upright more often than on its side. A 1920×1080 screen (1280×720 UI units) shown 390 CSS pixels wide draws a UI unit at 0.3 pixels: the chat's 15-unit letters are 4.6 pixels tall, and on its side (844×390) 8 pixels. Taps on the comment field did not open the phone's keyboard at the first tap, and nothing scrolled with a finger. Measured in Chrome at 390×844, device pixel ratio 3, a four-times slowed CPU and a hardware GPU, the lobby drew at 17 frames a second (Draw 40 ms on average), most of it filling circles (the affection dots, the avatars' backs) as vector paths every frame.

## Decision

Only the stream screens choose their size from the window; every other screen keeps the game's screen as before.

- `Game.Layout` gets the window's inside in device-independent pixels. While the lobby or the watch view is the screen shown, with no talk or event over it, `streamView` chooses the size: a window taller than wide takes a portrait layout 360 to 600 UI units wide (a phone held upright: a unit to a CSS pixel); a wide window keeps the game's screen unless that draws a unit smaller than 0.9 pixels (a phone on its side), and then takes a UI of the window's shape at 0.9 pixels a unit (smaller under 760×420 units). The pixels follow the device's pixel ratio, at most three to a unit. The UI scale (`gfx.SetScale`, the pointer's conversion) follows, and goes back to the game's when another screen comes up. A 1366×650 browser window or larger keeps the game's screen.
- The layouts share their parts (cards, chat lines, captions, buttons) and change only where and how large they are. In the portrait layout the lobby stands the cards one under another across the screen, each with its names, tag and dots larger, and the first without its chat beside it; the watch view has the stage (16:9) under the bar, a band of information, then the chat column across the screen with the answers, the comment field, the tip and the reading-aloud button at the bottom. Its full screen moves the stage (still 16:9, so the puppets show the same part of themselves as on the page and the screen never shows more of their bodies) to the top in place of the bar and the information, and runs the chat under it on a darkened band. On its side the landscape layout fits the window, the chat column narrowed to 30% of the width (300 to 380 units). Every card of the lobby shows its show's line as a caption (two lines at most), the line of the beat that the watch view shows, kept over the chat beats after it.
- At 390 CSS pixels wide the chat is 15 CSS pixels, the watch view's caption 17, a card's caption 16 to 17 and its name 16; on the side (844×390) the chat is 13.5 and the caption 19.
- Touch: a finger that moves more than 12 units is a swipe and does not click when it lifts. The lobby's cards and the chat scroll with it and glide on when it lifts; the chat keeps its place while it is scrolled back. A browser opens its keyboard only for a field focused in a touch event, and Ebitengine focuses its hidden text field in the event after a session was asked for; so the comment field starts typing when the finger touches it, and the keyboard opens when it lifts. The send button and the name's button work while a phone's keyboard still composes; a tap elsewhere stops typing and acts.
- The bar's buttons (the languages, then the place's entries, ADR 0048) are laid out leftward from the gold; those that would push into the logo go to a second row of the bar, the languages together, and the lobby's cards start under it. The settings opened from the lobby take the stream screens' size and are a page under the stream bar, the list of settings as wide as the screen allows (at most 640 units).
- The circles of the stream screens are round rectangles, whose pictures `gfx` keeps; the reading-aloud speaker is painted once into its own picture.
- The script command `window W H` sets the window's inside, so the graphics tests take the portrait layout.

## Consequences

A stream game is readable on a phone without the author choosing a screen size. Its other screens, and every other game, are drawn as before. In the measurement above the lobby draws at 60 frames a second (Draw 9.4 ms on average), and the watch view at 60 (Draw 9.1 ms, from 25 ms). A replay or a script clicks in UI units, so a run recorded in one window shape plays its clicks in another only as long as both choose the same layout.
